You can design the perfect survey and still lose half your audience — not because the questions were bad, but because they were in the wrong language. If a chunk of your customers or citizens are more comfortable in Urdu, an English-only form is quietly costing you responses before anyone even reads a question.

FeedbackPro now supports Urdu fonts across surveys, so questions, answer choices, and instructions can display cleanly in the language people actually think in.

## Why Language Changes the Answer

Even respondents who understand English reasonably well tend to open up more — and answer more accurately — in their native language. Offering Urdu:

-   Lifts completion rates

-   Cuts down on misread questions and stray answers

-   Makes people more comfortable answering honestly

-   Gets you longer, more useful written feedback

For public sector teams especially, this isn’t a nice-to-have — it’s the difference between hearing from your whole community and only hearing from the part of it that’s comfortable in English.

## Where It Matters Most

Think of a municipal office collecting feedback on public services. In English only, they hear from a slice of residents. Add Urdu, and suddenly the responses actually represent the community — including the service issues that were never getting reported before simply because no one asked in a language people were comfortable answering in.

The same logic applies to any business serving Urdu-speaking customers: same survey, more honest answers, once the language stops being the obstacle.

## Getting the Most Out of Urdu Surveys

-   **Keep it simple.** Plain, accessible Urdu beats formal or overly literary phrasing.

-   **Check it on mobile.** Most respondents are answering from a phone — make sure the text renders properly at that size.

-   **Keep surveys short.** Length kills completion rates regardless of language.

-   **Test before you launch.** Different devices and browsers can render fonts differently — check before it’s live.

## Tl;dr:

FeedbackPro now supports Urdu fonts, so surveys can be read and answered in the language your customers or citizens actually use day to day. Same survey, wider reach, better data — because the questions finally sound like something they’d say themselves.
